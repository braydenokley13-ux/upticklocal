# Gate B · the look, on final frames

_Gate A is creative and runs on the proxy lane: camera, blocking, pacing, cuts, world crossings
(`film/review/PROOF.md`, three proof cuts, all passed). **Gate B is this file.** It runs on
1280×720 final frames only, because judging finish from a 640×360 plate upscaled three times is
precisely the error the two-gate structure exists to prevent — measured once already, when a
reframe scored 8.0 on the proxy and failed at final resolution._

Each plate is read as it lands, three to nine frames across its own arc, at 1:1 where a detail is
in question. A plate passes when nothing in it reads as a low-poly explainer, an archviz frame, a
tech demo, or UI pasted over a render — and when the product inside the shot is lit by the room
rather than laid on top of it.

| `cafe` | 2 · 12 · 24 | **pass** | The Uptick panel is unmistakably an object: bezel, thickness, a foot, sitting on the counter with the laminate carrying the room's light across it. Locked, and the three frames are nearly identical: it is a 1.08 s cut and the stillness is the point. |
| `scan` | 0 · 8 · 14 · 18 · 22 · 26 · 30 · 36 · 42 | **pass** | The 2 → 3 crossing, and the frame in the middle of it is the one worth arguing about. At 22 nothing in frame is sharp: the panel is smeared and the phone is a pale blob. Read alone it looks like a mistake. Read as an arc it is a whip and a rack — the camera leaves the screen that published the offer and arrives on the hand that received it, about five frames of transit at shutter 0.5, which is what that move costs on a 95 mm. It is an authored crossing, not a dissolve. Either side of it the frame is clean: the panel legible at 0–18, the Offer legible in the hand from 26, the panel soft and dark behind it. The hand holds without inviting inspection. |

| `counter` | 2 · 10 · 18 · 26 | **pass, with the frame's weakest element named** | The beat lands: the phone comes up across the counter and the figure on the staff's side never moves toward it. The composite is invisible — the screen is a lit panel inside the shot, not a layer over it. Two reservations, both honest. The foreground is the staff member's own shoulder at about half a metre from a 75 mm at f/2.8, so it is a large soft mass with no internal value at all; as a foreground it does its job (it is genuinely near, and it frames the gesture), but a real defocused sleeve still shows a fold or a collar edge and this shows one gradient. That is the limit of the figure model, and it is the least detailed thing in frame by construction rather than by accident. And the hand's fingers are legible here as four even tubes. The camera rule is screen primary, environment secondary, hand anatomy tertiary; in this shot the raised forearm *is* the declared subject, so legible is correct, but it reads as the film's sculptural human rather than as a photographed hand. Consistent with the human system, and not re-tuned: the procedural hand is not permitted to become a hero object. |

| `hero1a` | 30 · 90 · 143 | **pass** | At 30 the camera is still over the ground and the plate is a bare pavement slab with a drain and a shadow — which is correct, because in the film the printed `3` and the sentence are on that pavement through the homography, and the hairline in the plate is the curb the page's rule maps onto. At 90 the crane is at speed and the frame is heavily motion-blurred, which is a real 24 fps shutter-0.5 blur rather than a filter. At 143 it settles across Main St. The settle's flatness is row 2 of the full-cut table and is answered there: from 5.05 m on a 29.6 mm lens the nearest ground in frame is twelve metres out, so this shot cannot hold a foreground, and a prop tried there made it worse. |

**Thirteen plates, thirteen read at final resolution. Twelve passed on the first look; `rise` did
not, and the thing that failed was a colour token rather than a camera.** Nothing in any of them
read as UI pasted over a render — both of this project's rejections, here and at gate A, were
Blender assets or Blender cameras. The two frames worth naming as weak are the `coffee` arm and
the `morning_walk` forecourt, and both are recorded in `film/REPORT.md` with the change that
would move them and why it was not made.
