# Hero 1 · punch list

_Critic round 5, on the A v4 preview frames (960×540 plate, pass-4 world) while the 1280×720 plate rendered. The lead's triage follows the list._

## Verdict

**6/10.** It clears the bar. The match-cut at 96→106 is real — the 3 and the sentence do not shift a pixel, the hairline is genuinely the curb, and the pavement beat (106–130) is the best thing in the shot: near-monochrome, blown, patient, and it earns the reveal. That alone puts it materially past a beige archviz plate with a shrinking number, and the story reads without narration: Joe's open and empty, ALDER packed with amber. But three rejected categories are still visible. Frames 140–160 are unreadable slab bands — pure previs. The buildings at the settle are flat-shaded boxes with painted-on windows and a Blender world-gradient sky behind them. And the settle doesn't settle: the camera is still craning at 251. Fix those and this is an 8.

## Punch list

1. `[PLATE] [P1] 196–251 — the "settle" is a continuous pull-back; the station shrinks and slides ~11% right between 220 and 251, so the shot ends mid-move and the outgoing cut will read as an accident — ease the crane to a dead stop by f228 with a 0.6% scale overshoot settling over 6 frames, then hold 228–251 with only the car and figures moving.`
2. `[PLATE] [P1] 196–251 — buildings are flat-shaded boxes with windows painted on at zero depth: the single loudest "3D test" signal in the film — recess every window 12 cm, add a glass plane at 0.05 roughness carrying a sky-gradient reflection, and give each roofline a 20 cm cornice so it throws a shadow line onto the facade.`
3. `[PLATE] [P1] 140–160 — mid-lift the frame is cream slabs and dark bands with nothing recognisable in it for ~20 frames; this is the previs stretch — front-load the elevation so the canopy line and ALDER awning silhouette are established by f150, then decelerate, and keep the camera outside the forecourt so columns never crop the frame into bands.`
4. `[COMPOSITE] [P1] 96–120 — the cream leaves as an opacity cross-fade (f106 is a visible 50% blend), which is the "ordinary dissolve" the brief forbids — instead hold the plate clipped at +2.5 stops on f96, ramp exposure down to 0 over f96–112 with highlights rolling off last, and cut the cream layer to 0 on a single frame at f97.`
5. `[PLATE] [P1] 196–251 — an amber figure stands in the live traffic lane beside the moving car, and a second, uncropped and shadowless, dominates the bottom-left corner at 220 — delete the road figure, and either remove the corner one or push it to a 30%-cropped, defocused foreground silhouette at the extreme bottom edge.`
6. `[COMPOSITE] [P1] 196–251 — type hierarchy collapses: the 3 in "3 JOE'S · 07:00–10:00" is set at caption cap-height, so the hero number reads as a bullet — set the 3 at 2.2× cap-height on the caption baseline, first word starting 0.75 em right, and stagger arrivals (caption f192, 3 stamps f200, "Same street." f224).`
7. `[PLATE] [P1] 106–130 — the sidewalk reads as tiled floor, not concrete: uniform 1.5 m grid, no aggregate, no wear — score joints at 1.2 m with a 4 mm chamfer that catches the sun, dirt the grooves, add one patched slab and a tar seam at the gutter, and chip the curb face with a 15 cm shadowed reveal.`
8. `[PLATE] [P1] 130–184 — the storm drain is a flat grey rectangle sticker — model a 6-slot grate recessed 40 mm with real AO in the slots, and hold it at roughly one-third from frame left through the lift so it works as the parallax anchor.`
9. `[PLATE] [P2] 196–251 — the sky is an empty two-stop gradient, ~20% of frame, and it flattens the whole world — add 2% volumetric haze so the far end of the street loses contrast, and lift the horizon 300 K warmer than zenith.`
10. `[COMPOSITE] [P2] 130–184 — the printed 3 never responds to the world: uniform navy, hard vector edges, no wear, no shadow crossing it — multiply the plate luminance onto it, drop to 0.88 opacity, erode the edge 2 px with noise, and add a 1 px light lip on the sun side once it reaches paint weight at f150.`
11. `[PLATE] [P2] all — amber figures read as chess pawns: identical height, even spacing, no yaw variation — build 3 height variants at ±8%, randomise yaw, cluster the ALDER group 2+3+1 with one seated at a table, and give every figure a hard contact shadow.`
12. `[PLATE] [P2] 150–208 — pumps and cars read as toy blocks — add a catenary hose and nozzle to each pump (the single detail that sells scale), bevel all pump edges 2–3 cm, and rebuild the sedan as a 3-box silhouette with a raked tinted windscreen, wheel arches and a 15 cm shadow gap.`
13. `[COMPOSITE] [P2] 30–96 — the page holds 2.5 s with a number and no sentence, then repeats "07:00–10:00" three times across header, sentence and mark — cut the header's second line, bring the sentence in at f42 over 10 frames, and lay a 2–3% paper tooth over the whole page so it reads as printed, not vector.`
14. `[PLATE] [P2] 106–160 — a soft unmotivated diagonal shaft crosses the concrete like a lens artefact — replace it with the hard-edged shadow of the canopy's leading edge, sun angle 1.5–2° for a believable penumbra, plus a warm bounce strip along the curb.`
15. `[COMPOSITE] [P2] 196–251 — the brick is candy-orange and the shadows are muddy brown — pull brick saturation 15–20%, add a 6500 K sky fill at 0.4 so shadows go blue-grey, and set "Same street. Same morning." in cream #F1EFE9 at 92% rather than pure white.`
16. `[PRODUCTION] [P3] 150–251 — the car strobes without renderer motion blur, crisp vector type sits over a soft 2× upscale, and the price sign reads "DIET" — enable 0.5 shutter, render 1920 native, relabel "REG / PREM", and run one grain pass over type and plate together at equal density.`

## Keep

1. **The match cut.** The 3 and the sentence hold their exact position and scale across 96→106. Do not re-time, re-scale or re-place either.
2. **The pavement beat, 106–130.** Near-monochrome, blown to paper, almost nothing happening. Its restraint is why the reveal lands.
3. **The casting of amber.** ALDER crowded, Joe's forecourt empty but open, one car at the pump. That's the whole argument of the film in one frame — don't populate Joe's.
